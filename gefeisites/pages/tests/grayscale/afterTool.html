How to test

  1. Let a recently powered-on display warm up. Use normal brightness and sit directly in front of it.
  2. Enter fullscreen with the button or F. Select each gray level: 5%, 10%, 25%, 50%, 75%, and 100%.
  3. At every level, scan the center, edges, and corners for fixed cloudy patches, vertical bands, or large color shifts. Keep your head position steady.
  4. Note the levels and positions that show a problem, then compare with ordinary scenes such as a sky or a camera pan. Exit fullscreen after all six levels.

What counts as normal

Use these references for this visual check. They are not a manufacturer acceptance standard or a hardware certification.

  • The fields have no obvious fixed clouds, bands, or large tinted areas at the inspected levels. Any faint near-black variation does not distract you in ordinary content.

How to read your result

To check for dirty screen effect during sports, watch for bands that stay put as the camera pans across the ice or field. Then use the 50% grayscale test pattern to see if those bands show up in the same spots.

Cloudy dark patches or fixed bands
These suggest uneven brightness, often called dirty screen effect. Check whether they stay at the same position.
One region looks pink, green, or blue
Color uniformity or viewing angle may be involved. Retest with your head centered.
Only the darkest fields look uneven
Near-black behavior varies with the panel and warm-up. One pattern alone does not establish a defect.

This visual check does not measure brightness uniformity. Panel technology, angle, and environment affect what you see.

If you cannot get a result

  1. Wait for the panel to warm up, keep the same brightness and viewing angle, and repeat the affected levels.
  2. Check a plain real scene. Phone exposure, lens shading, and compression can create or exaggerate patches in a photo.
  3. On OLED, allow normal automatic maintenance according to the maker's instructions. Repeated manual refresh is not a test step.

Frequently asked questions

What is dirty screen effect on a monitor or TV?

Dirty screen effect is visible brightness or color variation across what should be an even field. It often looks like cloudy patches, vertical bands, or faint stains during camera pans, sports, and other flat scenes. Gray patterns make the variation easier to spot.

Is uneven 5% gray normal on an OLED screen?

Some near-black variation is common, especially on a new panel or at very low brightness. Judge it from your normal seat after warm-up and check whether it appears in real dark content. Severe bands, tint, or blotches that remain distracting may justify contacting the seller.

Should I run a pixel refresh before this OLED uniformity test?

Let the display complete its normal automatic maintenance first. Do not repeatedly run a manual panel refresh just to chase a test pattern because intensive refresh cycles can add wear. Follow the manufacturer's instructions if a real-content problem remains after normal use.

Why does gray uniformity change when I move my head?

LCD viewing angles can shift brightness and tint across the screen, particularly on large panels viewed from close range. Test from the center at your normal distance. If the patch moves or changes as you move, viewing angle is contributing to what you see.

Can a camera accurately capture dirty screen effect?

A photo can help document the location, but automatic exposure, lens shading, moire, and compression can add patterns that your eyes do not see. Use a locked exposure and compare the photo with the screen in person. Real content from your normal seat matters most.
