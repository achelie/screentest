How to test

  1. Dim the room and keep the brightness you use for dark games or films. Sit at your normal distance, facing the center.
  2. Select Enter fullscreen or press F to show the black field. Use Hide controls or H so the toolbar does not light up the edges.
  3. Inspect the edges and corners, then move your head slightly left and right. Note which bright patches stay fixed and which change with your angle.
  4. Compare suspicious patches with a dark scene you normally watch. Restore the controls and exit fullscreen when finished.

What counts as normal

Use these references for this visual check. They are not a manufacturer acceptance standard or a hardware certification.

  • From your normal seat at everyday brightness, no fixed bright patch distracts you in dark content. A little angle-dependent glow alone does not establish a fault.

How to read your result

‘Bleed screen’ is another name for backlight bleed. On a black screen, it looks like light leaking through near an LCD panel’s edges or corners.

A bright edge patch stays put
This is consistent with backlight bleed or uneven backlighting. Check whether it also matters in real content.
The glow changes with your head position
Viewing-angle glow may contribute. Retest from the center at your usual distance.
A photo looks much brighter than the screen
Automatic camera exposure can exaggerate the patch. Use your eyes as the first reference.

Panel type, room light, local dimming, and viewing angle change the result. This page does not measure contrast or establish a warranty threshold.

If you cannot get a result

  1. Dim reflections, hide the controls, and repeat at your normal brightness instead of judging only a maximum-brightness photo.
  2. Change your angle and distance slightly. Fixed patches and moving glow give different clues.
  3. If pure black hides the problem, try Grayscale. OLED has no LCD backlight, and near-black unevenness needs a gray pattern.

Frequently asked questions

Is this backlight bleed or IPS glow?

Backlight bleed usually stays in the same edge or corner. IPS glow changes with your head position, viewing distance, and angle. Look straight at the display, then move slightly left and right. A patch that changes a lot is more likely IPS glow than fixed bleed.

Why does backlight bleed look worse in a phone photo?

Phone cameras often brighten a dark scene with longer exposure, high ISO, and automatic processing. That can turn mild glow into a dramatic cloud. Lock exposure if possible and judge the display with your eyes at normal brightness before using a photo as evidence.

What brightness and room lighting should I use?

Test once in a dim room at your normal brightness. You can raise brightness for a stress check, but a maximum-brightness photo in total darkness does not represent most use. The useful question is whether you notice the patch in dark games or films from your normal seat.

Can this black screen test check an OLED display?

It can reveal raised blacks, stuck pixels, or unwanted glow, but OLED panels do not have an LCD backlight, so the issue is not backlight bleed. Use the grayscale test as well because OLED near-black uniformity problems can be easier to see on dark gray than pure black.

Should I return a monitor with visible backlight bleed?

Consider how visible it is in real content, your normal room, and your normal seat. Compare it with the seller's return terms and the maker's panel policy while the return window is open. A browser test can document the symptom, but it cannot decide warranty eligibility.
