Make sense of a result

A pattern helps you describe what you see. Repeat the same check before changing your settings.

A dot stays in the same place

Clean the screen with a soft cloth, then switch between white, black, red, green and blue. Note the position and which backgrounds make the dot visible. A mark that moves when you wipe the glass points to a different problem from a dot that stays in the same spot.

If you need to show someone, take a photo of the panel. A screenshot captures the image the computer sent. A defect in the physical panel can be visible to your eyes and absent from that file.

An edge glow changes when I move

Use your usual brightness and sit straight in front of the display. Look at the dark pattern, then move your head a little to one side. Note whether the glow shifts with your viewing angle or stays along the same edge.

Check a dark scene you actually watch or play. A phone camera can brighten a black screen far beyond what you see from your chair. Record the brightness setting and room lighting if you take a comparison photo.

Bleed Screen Test for Backlight Bleed
A swipe breaks near the bezel

Make a slow pass across the touch grid, including each corner. Finish the test and look at the missed cells. An untouched square can simply mean your finger skipped it. Start a fresh pass and cross the same area from another direction.

If the gap repeats, dry the glass and your hands and try again with the charger unplugged. Record which edge and direction cause the break. A mouse can preview the grid, but a touch check needs a finger on the actual screen.

A smooth shade turns into stripes

Choose a neutral gradient first and compare the transition in both directions. Look for a step that remains visible from your normal viewing distance. Repeat with a single color channel to see whether the same boundary stands out.

Change one display setting at a time and rerun the same pattern. If you are comparing two monitors, keep the browser zoom, brightness and viewing angle consistent. Those details make the comparison easier to reproduce.

Gradient Banding Test
A moving edge leaves a trail

Pick a target speed and follow the moving edge with your eyes. Note whether you see a dark smear, a bright outline or uneven jumps. Pause the target to check that the mark disappears when the object stops.

Keep that speed while comparing two monitor response settings. A bright outline that appears only on the faster setting is a useful observation. The animation shows how motion looks in this browser; it does not measure a certified panel response time.

Before you test

  1. Wipe the screen

    Dust and tiny smears love pretending to be failed pixels.

  2. Use one pattern

    Fullscreen removes tabs, wallpaper, and other visual noise.

  3. Change input or angle

    If the mark stays put, document it before the return window closes.

Screen test questions

Can a website really find a dead pixel?

It can make a bad pixel easier to see by filling the panel with clean colors. You still make the diagnosis. Dust, a scratch, and a stuck pixel can look alike, so clean the screen before you start.

Will these tests fix my screen?

No. They help you spot and document a problem. Avoid pressing or rubbing the panel. If the display is new, check the return window before trying any risky repair advice.

Do you upload screenshots or test results?

No. The test patterns are drawn in your browser and guided answers stay in the current page. There is no account, database, or upload step.

Should brightness be set to 100 percent?

Usually not. Start with the brightness you use every day. For backlight bleed, test again in a dark room at a moderate setting so an unrealistic maximum does not exaggerate the result.
