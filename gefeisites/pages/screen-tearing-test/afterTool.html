How to test

  1. Use your normal resolution and refresh rate, keep browser zoom fixed, and move the window to the test display.
  2. Choose Stripe Tracking and a speed: Slow 240, Medium 520, or Fast 960 px/s. Use Play if the pattern is paused; the fullscreen button alone does not start it.
  3. Watch sharp moving edges for a horizontal break. Repeat with Block Layers and Checker Scroll at the same speed.
  4. Compare one sync or frame-limit setting at a time. Choose Pause after each observation and repeat the relevant setting in the game where you see the problem.

What counts as normal

How to read your result

A horizontal split offsets part of the image
That is consistent with tearing: different screen regions show different moments.
The entire pattern jumps or stops together
This looks more like stutter or uneven frame timing than tearing.
An edge leaves a dark trail or bright halo
That points toward ghosting or overdrive. Use the Motion test to compare it.
The browser frame number differs from monitor Hz
The number estimates animation callbacks. It is not a hardware refresh-rate reading or a VRR certificate.

The browser usually synchronizes composition. This page cannot turn VSync off, certify VRR operation, or reproduce every game's fullscreen and presentation behavior.

If you cannot get a result

  1. Choose Play if nothing moves. Returning from a hidden tab or a reduced-motion preference can leave the pattern paused.
  2. Close heavy tasks and keep the page in the foreground. Retest the same pattern and speed before blaming the panel.
  3. If the browser is smooth but a game tears, check VSync, FreeSync/G-Sync, and frame limits inside that game and its driver settings.

Screen tearing test FAQ

What does screen tearing look like?

A horizontal seam cuts through a moving image, with the content above and below the seam shifted sideways. The seam may stay near one area or jump between frames. It is easiest to spot on sharp vertical edges moving quickly across the screen.

Why does my game tear when this browser test looks smooth?

Browsers normally send animation through a compositor that synchronizes drawing with the display. A game can use a different fullscreen mode, frame rate, graphics API, and sync path. A clean browser result therefore does not prove the game is tear-free; repeat the comparison inside the game.

Why can tearing remain after I enable FreeSync or G-Sync?

The game frame rate may be outside the monitor's variable refresh range, a frame limiter may be missing, the game may be on the wrong display, or the driver and monitor settings may not match. Check the complete setup and keep the frame rate inside the display's VRR range.

Does a high refresh rate completely remove screen tearing?

No. A higher refresh rate makes each torn frame visible for less time, so tearing can be harder to notice, but unsynchronized frame delivery can still produce a seam. VSync or working VRR addresses timing; refresh rate alone does not guarantee synchronization.

What is the difference between VSync, VRR, and a frame-rate limit?

VSync schedules frame presentation around fixed display refreshes. VRR lets a compatible display vary its refresh timing within a supported range. A frame-rate limit caps how quickly frames are produced. They solve related timing problems but are not interchangeable, and many setups use a combination.

How can I tell tearing, stutter, and ghosting apart?

Tearing is a sideways break across part of one frame. Stutter makes the whole moving pattern pause or jump. Ghosting leaves a trail, dark smear, or bright halo behind moving edges. Use the Motion Test when the edge itself looks smeared rather than split.

Can a webpage verify VRR or turn VSync off?

No. A webpage cannot reliably disable browser compositor synchronization or certify that FreeSync or G-Sync is active. This tool provides repeatable motion for visual comparison, while the final check should use the game, driver overlay, and monitor indicators you actually rely on.
