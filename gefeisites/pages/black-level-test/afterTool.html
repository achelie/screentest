How to test

  1. Use SDR, normal brightness, and 100% browser zoom. Dim reflections and keep night mode and dynamic contrast off.
  2. Enter fullscreen, choose Pure black, hide controls, and let your eyes adapt for about 30 seconds. Inspect it from your usual seat for raised black or fixed bright regions.
  3. Switch to Near-black 1-10 and record which RGB 1–10 fields you can reliably separate from black. These are integer RGB values, not percentages.
  4. Choose Black depth and inspect the embedded dark fields and fine stripes. Compare local dimming on and off if available, then exit fullscreen and record the setting used.

What counts as normal

Use these references for this visual check. They are not a manufacturer acceptance standard or a hardware certification.

  • Use stable separation of dark fields as a relative reference between settings. There is no universal required count of visible RGB 1–10 fields; the room and panel matter.

How to read your result

All near-black fields disappear
Black level, gamma, mismatched output range, or the panel's near-black limits may hide detail.
Pure black looks gray
Reflections, an output-range mismatch, or the panel's native black level may raise it.
Dark blocks show but fine stripes disappear
Processing or local dimming may remove very dark fine detail. Compare with one setting changed.

These fixed SDR RGB patterns do not measure black luminance, contrast, nits, or panel type. Results support comparison rather than a universal acceptance score.

If you cannot get a result

  1. Reduce reflections and repeat at the same seating position and brightness. Do not change the room and display settings together.
  2. Check that the GPU and display agree on Full 0–255 or Limited 16–235 output. A mismatch can lift black or swallow shadows.
  3. Compare local dimming and contrast enhancements one at a time. If details remain unclear, note the lowest consistently visible value rather than inventing a pass count.

Black level test FAQ

How many near-black blocks should I be able to see?

There is no universal pass number for every monitor, room, and viewing mode. Count the blocks you can see consistently, change one setting, and compare again. The pattern is useful for relative adjustment, not a panel grade.

What is black crush?

Black crush happens when different dark input values are rendered as the same black output. Hair, fabric, game shadows, and dark movie scenes then lose detail even though the source still contains it.

Should HDR be off for this test?

Yes for a controlled SDR pass. HDR tone mapping can redistribute near-black values and make results vary by operating system, browser, and display mode. Test HDR separately with HDR content and the HDR tool.

Can RGB Full and Limited change the result?

Yes. A mismatch between 0-255 Full range and 16-235 Limited range can lift black or clip shadows. Check that the graphics output, monitor, television, receiver, and console use compatible range settings.

Should I change brightness or contrast first?

Start from the display preset and black-level or brightness guidance for your model. On many desktop monitors, the brightness control mainly changes backlight output rather than the digital black point. Change one control at a time and keep notes.

Is this the same as a backlight bleed test?

No. Both use black, but the Backlight Bleed Test focuses on fixed bright edges, cloudy corners, and IPS glow. This tool adds precise near-black steps and embedded shadow patterns to check tonal separation.

Can this page measure my monitor's black luminance or contrast ratio?

No. A browser can send RGB values, but it cannot measure the light leaving the panel. Black luminance, native contrast, and calibration accuracy require a meter and controlled conditions.
