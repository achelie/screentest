Backlight bleed appears as bright patches near the edge of an LCD when the screen should look black. It is easiest to see at night, which is also when people accidentally make the test far harsher than normal use.

## Set up a fair test

Use the room lighting you normally have while watching a dark film or playing a dark game. A completely black room with the display at maximum brightness can make a healthy panel look terrible.

Set brightness between 30 and 50 percent. Turn off local dimming for the first pass so it does not hide the panel's baseline behavior. Sit directly in front of the display at your usual distance.

## Show a black screen

Open the [dead pixel test](/tests/dead-pixel), choose black, and enter fullscreen. Wait 20 seconds for your eyes to settle. Look for fixed bright areas that hug an edge or corner.

Move your head slightly left and right. IPS glow usually changes with your viewing angle. Backlight bleed tends to stay anchored to the same edge in the same shape.

## Keep the camera honest

Phone cameras brighten dark scenes automatically. A long exposure can turn a mild glow into a white cloud. If you need a record, lower the phone exposure until the photo resembles what your eyes see.

Do not use the photo as the only verdict. Judge the panel during real content too. Watch a dark scene with subtitles or letterbox bars and note whether the bright area pulls your attention away from the picture.

## Check for pressure

An overtight monitor arm, twisted chassis, or pressure from packaging can affect the edge. Make sure the display sits flat and no cable is pushing against the rear casing. Do not loosen panel screws unless the manufacturer tells you to.

## When a return makes sense

Some edge glow is common on LCD panels. A return is reasonable when a patch is obvious at your normal brightness, in your normal room, from your normal seat. Save one honest photo, note the settings, and contact the seller while the return window is open.

OLED screens do not use an LCD backlight. Bright patches on an OLED need a different diagnosis, such as image retention, panel uniformity, or physical damage.
