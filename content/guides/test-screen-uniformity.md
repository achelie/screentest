Uniformity describes how evenly a screen shows the same tone from one edge to the other. Poor uniformity can look like cloudy patches, darker corners, colored bands, or a faint dirty layer during camera pans.

## Start with middle gray

Open the [grayscale test](/tests/grayscale) and choose a tone near 50 percent. View it fullscreen from your normal seat. Middle gray reveals brightness changes without the glare of a full white screen.

Let your eyes move across the panel instead of staring at one spot. Look for vertical bands, cloudy areas, and corners that fall darker than the center.

## Check more than one level

Repeat the check at 5, 25, 50, 75, and 100 percent gray. A panel can look even at one level and uneven at another.

Use white to check edge brightness and color tint. Use dark gray to check near-black uniformity. On OLED, very dark gray may reveal vertical bands that are invisible during bright content.

## Add solid colors

Run the [color test](/tests/color) and pause on red, green, and blue. A patch that looks neutral on gray but changes color on these fields may be a tint or subpixel issue rather than simple brightness variation.

Keep any color management mode consistent during the test. Switching picture presets halfway through makes comparison useless.

## Try real movement

Uniformity problems often stand out when a flat scene moves. Use the [motion test](/tests/motion) with a pale background and watch for stationary bands behind the moving object. Sports fields, skies, and slow game camera pans are common real-world checks.

## Decide whether it matters

No large panel is perfectly uniform. The useful question is whether you notice the issue from your seat during content you actually use.

Document a problem with one gray photo and one real scene. Keep camera exposure close to what your eyes see. If a new display has a strong central band, obvious colored tint, or distracting dirty screen effect, contact the seller before the return window closes.
